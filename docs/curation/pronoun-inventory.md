# Pronoun inventory

The checklist for `/curriculum/pronouns`. Every concept here carries `topic:pronoun`
plus `pos:pronoun` (or `pos:function-word` for the determiner senses) and a
`grammar:` subcategory. `es:` / `en:` lemma tags let the page answer "every Spanish
form of *her*" (`en:her`) and "every English sense of *su*" (`es:su`).

## Personal — subject  ✅
I · you · he · she · it · we · they  ←  yo, tú·usted, él, ella, ello, nosotros·nosotras, ellos·ellas

## Personal — object  ✅
me · you · him · her · it · us · them  ←  me, te, lo·la·le, nos, los·las·les

## Personal — prepositional  (authored: explicit rows per preposition)
English uses the object form after every preposition; Spanish uses the tonic set
(mí, ti, él, ella, nosotros, ellos) and two fused irregulars.
- with: conmigo→with me, contigo→with you, con él→with him, con ella→with her, con nosotros→with us, con ellos→with them, con [alguien]→with [somebody]
- for: para mí→for me … para [alguien]→for [somebody]
- without: sin mí→without me … sin [alguien]→without [somebody]
- to (emphatic/clarifying): a mí→to me, a ti→to you, a él→to him, a ella→to her, a [alguien]→to [somebody]
- about/of: de mí→about me, de él→about him, de ella→about her, de [alguien]→about [somebody]

## Constructions where pronoun case shifts

`querer que [alguien] [haga algo]` and its ~30 siblings hide that Spanish uses a
subject pronoun + subjunctive while English uses an object pronoun + infinitive:
**Quiero que él lo haga → I want him to do it.** The subordinate-subject paradigm
(que yo → me to, que él → him to, …) is authored as its own concept family,
tagged `grammar:subordinate-subject-pronoun` + `construction:object-control`, and
the sibling frames are cross-tagged and have their examples fixed to show a real
pronoun.
