# Connector **Que**: **That** or No Separate Word

When **que** introduces a complete idea, English can often use connector **that**.

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Creo que es importante. | I think that it is important. |
| Sé que Laura está aquí. | I know that Laura is here. |

In everyday speech, **that** is often optional:

- **I think it is important.**
- **I know Laura is here.**

Some English verb structures do not use connector **that** at all:

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Quiero que Laura lo haga. | I want Laura to do it. |
| Necesito que Laura lo haga. | I need Laura to do it. |
| Le digo a Laura que lo haga. | I tell Laura to do it. |

In these patterns, English uses **person + to + base verb**, not *that + person + verb*.

Other high-frequency structures also contain no separate translation of **que**:

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Tengo que hacerlo. | I have to do it. |
| Hay que hacerlo. | You have to do it. / We have to do it. |

Learn **tener que → have to** as one complete obligation structure. Spanish impersonal **hay que** requires an English subject chosen from context.
