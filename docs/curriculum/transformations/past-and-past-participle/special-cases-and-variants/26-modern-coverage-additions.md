# Modern Coverage Additions and Regional Variants

This is a layer-one safety net for non-archaic forms that were missing from the three source folders. High-frequency forms should later receive their own gradual teaching sets; rare forms remain here so they are not silently filtered out.

| Base | Past | Past participle | Use or register |
|---|---|---|---|
| beget | begot | begotten / begot | formal: engendrar, producir |
| clad | clad / cladded | clad / cladded | **clad** is most common as a participle or adjective |
| cleave | cleaved / cleft / clove | cleaved / cleft / cloven | rare outside formal or literary uses; meaning affects the preferred form |
| crow | crowed / crew | crowed | **crew** is a rare past for a rooster crowing |
| dive | dived / dove | dived | **dove** is a common American past; **dived** is the standard participle |
| forsake | forsook | forsaken | abandonar |
| shit | shit / shat / shitted | shit / shat / shitted | vulgar; forms vary by dialect and speaker |
| smite | smote | smitten | formal, literary, or humorous |
| sneak | sneaked / snuck | sneaked / snuck | **snuck** is especially common in American English |
| stave | staved / stove | staved / stove | **stove** is rare; **staved off** is the ordinary current form |
| strew | strewed | strewn / strewed | **strewn** remains current: **The papers were strewn everywhere.** |
| sweat | sweated / sweat | sweated / sweat | **sweated** is the safest general form |

## Useful first-pass contrasts

- **She dove into the pool yesterday, but she has never dived from that height before.** — Se lanzó a la piscina ayer, pero nunca se ha lanzado desde esa altura.
- **He snuck out, but he has never sneaked back in unnoticed.** — Se escapó, pero nunca ha vuelto a entrar a escondidas sin que lo noten.
- **They forsook the old plan, and the building has been forsaken for years.** — Abandonaron el plan anterior y el edificio lleva años abandonado.
- **The papers were strewn across the floor.** — Los papeles estaban esparcidos por el piso.

The uncommon and register-sensitive forms belong in the coverage record, not in the first memorization sequence.
