# Cognates: Spanish **-ano / -iano** and English **-an / -ian**

In these cognates, remove the final **-o** from Spanish **-ano** or **-iano** to reveal a related English form ending in **-an** or **-ian**. Some words require an additional spelling change.

| Spanish | English | Example |
|---|---|---|
| humano | **human** | **Todo ser humano necesita agua para vivir.**<br>Every human being needs water to live. |
| urbano | **urban** | **El transporte urbano mejora la conexión entre los barrios.**<br>Urban transportation improves connections between neighborhoods. |
| republicano | **republican** | **El país adoptó un sistema republicano.**<br>The country adopted a republican system. |
| africano | **African** | **El museo exhibe arte africano contemporáneo.**<br>The museum exhibits contemporary African art. |
| americano | **American** | **El inglés americano y el británico tienen algunas diferencias.**<br>American and British English have some differences. |
| colombiano | **Colombian** | **El café colombiano se exporta a muchos países.**<br>Colombian coffee is exported to many countries. |
| italiano | **Italian** | **El restaurante sirve comida italiana.**<br>The restaurant serves Italian food. |
| cristiano | **Christian** | **La iglesia pertenece a una comunidad cristiana.**<br>The church belongs to a Christian community. |

## Meaning Distinction

English normally uses **American** for someone or something from the United States. Spanish **americano** can refer more broadly to the Americas, so **estadounidense** is more precise for the United States.
