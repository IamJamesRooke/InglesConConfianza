# Cognates: Spanish **-ar** and English **-ar**

These Spanish and English cognates end in **-ar**, although some require a spelling adjustment.

| Spanish | English | Example |
|---|---|---|
| dólar | **dollar** | **El precio aumentó un dólar esta semana.**<br>The price increased by one dollar this week. |
| regular | **regular** | **El restaurante recibe clientes regulares cada viernes.**<br>The restaurant receives regular customers every Friday. |
| popular | **popular** | **Este parque es popular entre las familias.**<br>This park is popular with families. |
| similar, parecido | **similar** | **Los dos diseños tienen una estructura similar.**<br>The two designs have a similar structure. |
| espectacular | **spectacular** | **Desde la montaña vimos un paisaje espectacular.**<br>We saw a spectacular landscape from the mountain. |

## Additional Cognates

| Spanish | English | Example |
|---|---|---|
| circular | **circular** | **La mesa tiene una forma circular.**<br>The table has a circular shape. |
| particular | **particular** | **Necesitamos resolver un problema particular.**<br>We need to solve a particular problem. |
| singular | **singular** | **El verbo está en la tercera persona del singular.**<br>The verb is in the third person singular. |

## False Cognates

Spanish **familiar** normally means related to the family or can refer to a relative. English **familiar** means **conocido** or **reconocible**.

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| **Un familiar me acompañó al hospital.** | A **relative** accompanied me to the hospital. |
| **Esa canción me resulta conocida.** | That song sounds **familiar** to me. |

## Informal alternative

**Dollar** is standard in every context. Informal **buck** usually refers to one dollar or dollars in everyday conversation.

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| El libro cuesta veinte dólares. | The book costs twenty dollars. |
| Ese libro cuesta veinte dólares. | That book costs twenty bucks. |
