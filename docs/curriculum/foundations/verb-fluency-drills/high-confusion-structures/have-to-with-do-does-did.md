# Fluency Drill: **Have To** with **Do, Does**, and **Did**

Use **do, does**, or **did** to form questions and negatives with **have to**.

| Time and subject | Spanish | Question | Affirmative answer | Negative answer |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| present: **you** | ¿Tienes que hacerlo? | Do you have to do it? | Yes, I have to do it. | No, I don't have to do it. |
| present: **she** | ¿Ella tiene que hacerlo? | Does she have to do it? | Yes, she has to do it. | No, she doesn't have to do it. |
| past: **they** | ¿Ellos tenían que hacerlo? | Did they have to do it? | Yes, they had to do it. | No, they didn't have to do it. |

After **does** and **did**, use base **have**:

- **Does she have to do it?**, not *Does she has to do it?*
- **Did they have to do it?**, not *Did they had to do it?*

Compare **have got to**, which is limited to present obligation:

| Form | Example |
|---|---|
| full form | I have got to do it. |
| contraction | I've got to do it. |
| very informal speech | I gotta do it. |

Use **had to**, not *had got to*, for past obligation.
