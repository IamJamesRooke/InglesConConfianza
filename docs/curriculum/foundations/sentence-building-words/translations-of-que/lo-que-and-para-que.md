# **Lo Que** and **Para Que**

## **Lo que → what**

Use **what** for the thing or idea expressed by **lo que**.

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Esto es lo que necesito. | This is what I need. |
| Quiero saber lo que piensas. | I want to know what you think. |

Do not use **that** here: **what I need**, not *that I need* when the meaning is **lo que necesito**.

## **Lo cual → which**

**Lo cual** normally refers back to an entire previous idea and is more formal than **lo que**.

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| La reunión fue cancelada, lo cual entiendo. | The meeting was canceled, which I understand. |

Here **which** refers to the fact that the meeting was canceled. It does not ask a question or mean **cuál**.

## **Para que → so that**

Use **so that** to introduce an intended result with its own subject.

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Hablo despacio para que puedas entender. | I speak slowly so that you can understand. |
| Te llamo para que sepas dónde estoy. | I am calling you so that you know where I am. |

When the person performing both actions is the same, English normally uses **to**:

**Voy a llamar para confirmar la reunión.**<br>
I am going to call to confirm the meeting.
