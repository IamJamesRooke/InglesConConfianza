# Conditional Meaning and Form

English conditional sentences connect a condition with its result. The verb forms already taught in the fluency drills combine differently according to whether the speaker presents the situation as general, possible, hypothetical, or impossible to change.

| Meaning | Condition | Result | Master sentence |
|---|---|---|---|
| general result | present | present | If I have enough time, I finish the report carefully. |
| real future possibility | present | **will + base verb** | If I have enough time tomorrow, I will finish the report. |
| hypothetical present or future | past form | **would + base verb** | If I had enough time, I would finish the report. |
| impossible past | **had + past participle** | **would have + past participle** | If I had had enough time, I would have finished the report. |

The traditional names—**zero, first, second**, and **third conditional**—are useful labels. The meaning and verb-form contrast are more important than the number.
