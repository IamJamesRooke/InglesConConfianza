# Uses of “It”

English uses `it` for objects, animals, ideas, situations and impersonal expressions.

Spanish does not have one direct equivalent of `it`. The translation changes according to:

- the grammatical gender of the noun;
- whether `it` is the subject, direct object or indirect object;
- whether it follows a preposition;
- whether it refers to a specific thing or an entire situation;
- whether English uses `it` without referring to anything.

# Subject

Spanish normally omits the subject pronoun when the object is already understood.

## Masculine Noun

| Full context | Natural Spanish | English |
|---|---|---|
| El libro es nuevo. | Es interesante. | The book is new. It is interesting. |
| El robot está aquí. | Está funcionando. | The robot is here. It is working. |
| El teléfono está en la mesa. | Es mío. | The phone is on the table. It is mine. |

Spanish does not normally say `Él es interesante` when referring to a book. Once the object is established, the subject is usually omitted.

## Feminine Noun

| Full context | Natural Spanish | English |
|---|---|---|
| La mesa es nueva. | Es grande. | The table is new. It is big. |
| La casa está lejos. | Es muy bonita. | The house is far away. It is very beautiful. |
| La máquina está aquí. | Está funcionando. | The machine is here. It is working. |

# Direct Object

The direct-object translation of `it` depends on the grammatical gender of the noun.

## Masculine: Lo

| Reference | Full context | Pronoun form | English |
|---|---|---|---|
| 📕 el libro | Veo el libro. | Lo veo. | I see the book. I see it. |
| 🤖 el robot | Veo el robot. | Lo veo. | I see the robot. I see it. |
| el teléfono | Tengo el teléfono. | Lo tengo. | I have the phone. I have it. |
| el problema | Entiendo el problema. | Lo entiendo. | I understand the problem. I understand it. |

## Feminine: La

| Reference | Full context | Pronoun form | English |
|---|---|---|---|
| la mesa | Veo la mesa. | La veo. | I see the table. I see it. |
| la casa | Veo la casa. | La veo. | I see the house. I see it. |
| la respuesta | Sé la respuesta. | La sé. | I know the answer. I know it. |
| la máquina | Necesito la máquina. | La necesito. | I need the machine. I need it. |

## Minimal Difference

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Veo el libro. Lo veo. | I see the book. I see it. |
| Veo la mesa. La veo. | I see the table. I see it. |

English uses `it` in both sentences. Spanish uses `lo` for the masculine noun and `la` for the feminine noun.

# Indirect Object

`Le` can translate as `to it` or `for it` when the recipient is a singular animal, object, machine or organization.

| Reference | Full context | Pronoun form | English |
|---|---|---|---|
| 🤖 el robot | Doy una instrucción al robot. | Le doy una instrucción. | I give the robot an instruction. I give it an instruction. |
| 🐕️ el perro | Doy comida al perro. | Le doy comida. | I give the dog food. I give it food. |
| la empresa | Envío el documento a la empresa. | Le envío el documento. | I send the company the document. I send it the document. |
| la máquina | Hago una reparación a la máquina. | Le hago una reparación. | I make a repair to the machine. I make a repair to it. |

`Le` does not indicate grammatical gender.

# After a Preposition

After a preposition, Spanish can use `él` or `ella` for a previously identified object.

## Masculine: Él

| Full context | Pronoun form | English |
|---|---|---|
| El proyecto es importante. Trabajo en el proyecto. | Trabajo en él. | The project is important. I work on it. |
| El libro está aquí. Hablo sobre el libro. | Hablo sobre él. | The book is here. I speak about it. |
| El problema es complicado. Pienso en el problema. | Pienso en él. | The problem is complicated. I think about it. |

## Feminine: Ella

| Full context | Pronoun form | English |
|---|---|---|
| La aplicación es importante. Trabajo en la aplicación. | Trabajo en ella. | The application is important. I work on it. |
| La idea es interesante. Hablo sobre la idea. | Hablo sobre ella. | The idea is interesting. I speak about it. |
| La situación es complicada. Pienso en la situación. | Pienso en ella. | The situation is complicated. I think about it. |

# Reflexive Uses

When an object or animal performs an action on itself, English uses `itself`. Spanish normally uses `se`.

| Reference | Spanish | English |
|---|---|---|
| 🤖 | El robot se ve en el espejo. | The robot sees itself in the mirror. |
| 🐕️ | El perro se rasca. | The dog scratches itself. |
| la máquina | La máquina se apaga. | The machine turns itself off. |

# Combined Object Pronouns

In combined forms, one English `it` may represent the object being transferred, while another `it` may represent the recipient.

## Masculine Direct Object: Lo

| Full context | Combined form | English |
|---|---|---|
| Doy el libro al robot. | Se lo doy. | I give the robot the book. I give it to it. |
| Envío el documento a la empresa. | Se lo envío. | I send the company the document. I send it to it. |
| Doy el juguete al perro. | Se lo doy. | I give the dog the toy. I give it to it. |

In these sentences:

- `se` represents the recipient;
- `lo` represents the masculine object being given or sent.

## Feminine Direct Object: La

| Full context | Combined form | English |
|---|---|---|
| Doy la llave al robot. | Se la doy. | I give the robot the key. I give it to it. |
| Envío la respuesta a la empresa. | Se la envío. | I send the company the answer. I send it to it. |
| Doy la comida al perro. | Se la doy. | I give the dog the food. I give it to it. |

In these sentences:

- `se` represents the recipient;
- `la` represents the feminine object being given or sent.

# It as an Impersonal Subject

English frequently requires `it` even when the sentence does not refer to a specific object.

Spanish does not translate this `it`.

## Weather

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Llueve. | It is raining. |
| Está lloviendo. | It is raining. |
| Hace frío. | It is cold. |
| Hace calor. | It is hot. |

## Time

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Es la una. | It is one o’clock. |
| Son las tres. | It is three o’clock. |
| Es tarde. | It is late. |
| Es temprano. | It is early. |

## General Statements

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Es importante. | It is important. |
| Es difícil. | It is difficult. |
| Es posible. | It is possible. |
| Parece fácil. | It seems easy. |
| No importa. | It does not matter. |

In these sentences, English needs a grammatical subject. Spanish does not add a pronoun corresponding to `it`.

# It Referring to an Idea or Situation

When `it` refers to an entire idea, statement or situation, Spanish often uses `eso`, `esto` or no explicit pronoun.

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Eso es importante. | It is important. / That is important. |
| No entiendo eso. | I do not understand it. |
| Esto es difícil. | It is difficult. / This is difficult. |
| No lo entiendo. | I do not understand it. |
| Ya sé. | I already know it. / I already know. |

# Summary

| Use of “it” | Spanish form |
|---|---|
| understood subject | usually omitted |
| masculine direct object | lo |
| feminine direct object | la |
| singular indirect object | le |
| after a preposition, masculine | él |
| after a preposition, feminine | ella |
| reflexive | se |
| masculine object in a combined form | lo |
| feminine object in a combined form | la |
| impersonal subject | not translated |
| idea or situation | eso, esto, lo or omitted |
