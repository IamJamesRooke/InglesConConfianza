# Uses of **Too**

> **The two tailors are too tired to tailor two tutus too.**

**Too** expresses excess or means **also**. Position and context distinguish the two meanings.

## **Too + Description** Meaning Excess

Use **too** when a degree is greater than wanted, acceptable or possible.

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Estoy demasiado ocupado para revisar el informe hoy. | I'm too busy to review the report today. |

**Too busy** presents the busyness as a problem. It does not simply mean **very busy**.

## **Too Much** and **Too Many**

Spanish translates directly:

- **demasiado tráfico → too much traffic**
- **demasiados carros → too many cars**

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Hay demasiado tráfico y demasiados carros como para que lleguemos antes de las ocho. | There's too much traffic and there are too many cars for us to arrive before eight. |

## **Too...To**

Use **too + description + to + base verb** when an excessive degree prevents or limits an action.

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| El archivo es demasiado grande para enviarlo por correo. | The file is too large to send by email. |
| Estoy demasiado cansada para conducir. | I'm too tired to drive. |

The action after **to** uses the base verb: **too tired to drive**.

## **Too...For Someone To**

Name the person affected by placing **for + person** before the full infinitive.

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| El informe es demasiado técnico para que el cliente lo entienda sin ayuda. | The report is too technical for the client to understand without help. |

## **Much Too** Versus **Too Much**

Use **much too** before a description and **too much** before a thing or activity.

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| El apartamento es demasiado caro y cuesta demasiado para nuestro presupuesto. | The apartment is much too expensive, and it costs too much for our budget. |

- **much too expensive**
- **too much money**
- **work too much**

Do not say *too much expensive*.

## **Far Too** and Informal **Way Too**

**Far too** and **way too** strengthen the idea of excess.

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Dos horas es demasiado tiempo para una reunión que podría durar veinte minutos. | Two hours is far too long for a meeting that could take twenty minutes. |

Informal conversation commonly uses **way too long**. Prefer **far too long** or simply **too long** in formal writing.

## **Not Too**

**Not too + description** often means **not very**, especially in polite or understated speech.

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| El examen no estuvo muy difícil, así que terminé temprano. | The exam wasn't too difficult, so I finished early. |

In requests such as **Don't drive too fast**, **too fast** still means faster than acceptable.

## **Too** Meaning Also

At the end of a positive statement, **too** means **también**.

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Laura va a asistir y yo también voy. | Laura's going to attend, and I'm going too. |
| Yo también lo hago. | I do it too. |

This **too** does not express excess.

## **Me Too**

Use informal **me too** as a short agreement with a positive statement.

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| —Quiero ir a Cartagena. —Yo también. | “I want to go to Cartagena.” “Me too.” |

The fuller pattern repeats the matching short verb: **So do I**.

For negative agreement, neutral English uses **me neither** or a **neither** pattern:

- **I don't drive.**
- **Me neither. / Neither do I.**

## Formal or Emphatic **Too**

**Too** can appear directly after the person it emphasizes, usually with commas in writing.

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Laura también vio el error, aunque no dijo nada durante la reunión. | Laura, too, saw the error, although she said nothing during the meeting. |

This placement sounds more formal than **Laura saw the error too**.

## **All Too**

**All too** means regrettably or extremely familiar, common or easy.

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Es demasiado común que una contraseña débil permita el acceso a datos privados. | It's all too common for a weak password to expose private data. |

The phrase often carries a negative judgment.

## **Only Too**

Formal **only too** means very or more than willing, aware or pleased.

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Con mucho gusto revisaré el contrato antes de que lo firmes. | I'll be only too happy to review the contract before you sign it. |

This does not mean “excessively happy” in a negative sense.

## **Cannot Be Too Careful**

**You cannot be too careful** is a special pattern meaning that any amount of care is justified.

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| No se puede ser demasiado cuidadoso al verificar un número de cuenta antes de transferir dinero. | You can't be too careful when checking an account number before transferring money. |

Although the sentence is negative, it recommends maximum care.

## Three Mastery Sentences

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Los dos sastres están demasiado cansados para confeccionar dos tutús también. | The two tailors are too tired to tailor two tutus too. |
| El material es demasiado caro y cuesta demasiado; además, el diseño es demasiado complejo para que lo terminemos hoy. | The material is much too expensive and costs too much; the design is far too complex for us to finish today too. |
| —Yo también revisaré las cifras. —Yo también; nunca se puede ser demasiado cuidadoso. | “I'll check the figures too.” “Me too; you can't be too careful.” |

Together, these sentences contrast excess **too**, **too...to**, **too much**, **much/far too**, **too** meaning also, **me too**, the homophones **to/two**, and the special maximum-care pattern.
