# Prepositional **To**

> **I'm looking forward to going to the meeting to talk to the two people I sent the proposal to.**

Prepositional **to** points toward a destination, recipient, endpoint or relationship. If a verb follows this **to**, the PING rule requires **-ing**.

## Destination

Use **to** for movement toward a destination.

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Voy a la tienda y después regreso a la oficina. | I'm going to the store and then going back to the office. |

Common movement chunks include:

- **go / come / walk / run / drive / fly / travel to**
- **get to** a place
- **take** someone or something **to** a place
- **return / go back / come back to**

Do not add **to** before **home, here**, or **there**:

- **go home**, not *go to home*;
- **come here**, not *come to here*;
- **drive there**, not *drive to there*.

## **To** Versus **Toward**

**To** normally presents the destination as the endpoint. **Toward** or **towards** presents the direction and does not guarantee arrival.

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Caminé hacia la estación, pero antes de llegar decidí volver a casa. | I walked toward the station, but before I got there, I decided to go home. |
| Caminé a la estación y tomé el tren de las ocho. | I walked to the station and took the eight o'clock train. |

Both **toward** and **towards** are standard. **Toward** is more common in American English; **towards** is more common in British English.

## **Arrive, Reach**, and **Enter**

These destination verbs do not follow the ordinary **go to** pattern.

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Llegamos al hotel, entramos al edificio y llegamos a la recepción antes de las nueve. | We arrived at the hotel, entered the building, and reached reception before nine. |

- **arrive at** a specific place; **arrive in** a city or country;
- **reach + place**, not *reach to*;
- **enter + place**, not *enter to*.

## Recipient

Use **to** before the person or group receiving something.

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Le envié las dos versiones a Laura y le expliqué los cambios a su equipo. | I sent both versions to Laura and explained the changes to her team. |

Compare the two natural orders:

- **I sent Laura the file.**
- **I sent the file to Laura.**

Pronouns follow the same pattern: **Send it to me**, not *send me it* in the core pattern taught here.

## Spanish Personal **A** Does Not Become **To**

Spanish uses personal **a** before many people who receive the direct action. English does not translate it.

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Vi a Laura y llamé a Carlos. | I saw Laura and called Carlos. |

Do not say *I saw to Laura* or *I called to Carlos* for these meanings.

## Verb + **To** Combinations

Some English verbs include **to** before the person, thing or idea connected to the action.

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Escucha a Laura y habla con ella antes de responderle al cliente. | Listen to Laura and talk to her before you respond to the client. |
| Le expliqué el problema al gerente, pero la solución le pertenece al equipo técnico. | I explained the problem to the manager, but the solution belongs to the technical team. |
| ¿Qué le pasó al archivo que enviamos? | What happened to the file we sent? |

High-use combinations include:

- **listen to**
- **talk / speak to**
- **explain / describe something to someone**
- **belong to**
- **happen to**
- **respond / reply to**
- **refer to**
- **apologize to** someone
- **object to**
- **admit / confess to**

Do not copy Spanish word order into **explain**: **Explain it to me**, not *explain me it*.

## **Been To** a Place

Use **have been to** when someone has visited a place and returned or has experience visiting it.

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| He estado en Medellín dos veces, pero nunca he vivido allá. | I've been to Medellín twice, but I've never lived there. |

Compare:

- **Laura has gone to Medellín**: she went and is still away;
- **Laura has been to Medellín**: she visited at some time and returned.

## Endpoint, Limit and Range

**To** can mark the endpoint of time, distance, a range or a change.

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Trabajo de ocho a cinco y camino de la casa a la oficina. | I work from eight to five and walk from home to the office. |
| La temperatura subió de diez a veinte grados. | The temperature rose from ten to twenty degrees. |
| Faltan diez minutos para las dos. | It's ten to two. |

## Relationship, Preference and Comparison

**To** can connect one thing to another.

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Prefiero caminar a conducir porque la solución al tráfico no es agregar más carros a la avenida. | I prefer walking to driving because the solution to traffic isn't adding more cars to the avenue. |

Useful combinations include:

- **prefer X to Y**
- **similar to**
- **equal to**
- **married to**
- **related to**
- **the answer / key / solution to**
- **an invitation to**
- **damage to**

Do not use *than* after **prefer** in this pattern: **I prefer walking to driving**.

## The PING Rule With **To**

When **to** is a preposition and a verb follows, use **verb-ing**:

**prepositional to + ING**

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Espero con ganas volver a verlos. | I'm looking forward to seeing you again. |
| Ella se opone a trabajar los domingos. | She objects to working on Sundays. |
| Estamos comprometidos con proteger los datos de los clientes. | We're committed to protecting client data. |

Common PING chunks include:

- **look forward to doing**
- **object to doing**
- **admit / confess to doing**
- **be committed / dedicated to doing**
- **be opposed to doing**
- **contribute to doing**
- **the key / solution to doing**
- **in addition to doing**

The crucial question is not “Does the sentence contain **to**?” It is “Is this **to** part of a full infinitive, or is it a preposition?”

## Full-Infinitive **To** Versus Prepositional **To**

| Full infinitive | Prepositional **to** |
|---|---|
| **I want to go.** | **I'm used to going.** |
| **I plan to work.** | **I object to working.** |
| **I called to confirm.** | **I'm committed to confirming every detail.** |

The word after full-infinitive **to** is the base verb. The word after prepositional **to** is a noun, pronoun or **-ing** action.

## **To Me** Versus **For Me**

Use **to me** for direction, communication, recipient, or personal perception. Use **for me** for benefit, purpose, suitability, or representation.

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Ella me explicó la regla y después hizo una copia para mí. | She explained the rule to me and then made a copy for me. |
| Para mí, la segunda opción parece más segura. | To me, the second option seems safer. |

## Three Mastery Sentences

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Espero con ganas ir a Medellín para hablar con las dos personas a quienes les envié la propuesta. | I'm looking forward to going to Medellín to talk to the two people I sent the proposal to. |
| Prefiero caminar hacia la oficina a conducir hasta allá, pero todavía no estoy acostumbrado a llegar caminando. | I prefer walking toward the office to driving there, but I'm still not used to arriving on foot. |
| Explícamelo antes de responderle al cliente porque la solución al problema puede pertenecerle al equipo que se opone a cambiar el sistema. | Explain it to me before replying to the client because the solution to the problem may belong to the team that objects to changing the system. |

Together, these sentences test destination, direction, recipient, final prepositions, verb combinations, relationship **to**, preference, and prepositional **to + -ing**.
