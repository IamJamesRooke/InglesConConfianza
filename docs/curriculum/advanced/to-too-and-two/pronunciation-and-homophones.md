# Pronunciation and Homophones: **To, Too**, and **Two**

> **Two tired tailors tried to tailor two tutus, too.**

**To, too**, and **two** are homophones when **to** is stressed: all three can sound `/tuː/`. In ordinary speech, unstressed **to** usually becomes the weaker `/tə/`.

## Strong `/tuː/`

Use the strong pronunciation when **to** is emphasized, contrasted or left at the end.

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| —No quiero ir. —Tienes que ir. | “I don't want to go.” “You have to.” |

The final **to** carries the understood meaning **to go**, so it remains strong.

## Reduced `/tə/`

In normal connected speech, **to** before a verb or destination is often unstressed.

- **I want to go** → approximately **I wanna go** in very informal speech;
- **I'm going to call** → approximately **I'm gonna call** in very informal speech;
- **I have got to leave** → approximately **I've gotta leave** in very informal speech.

**Wanna, gonna**, and **gotta** represent relaxed pronunciation. Use **want to, going to**, and **have got to** in standard writing.

Do not reduce **to** mechanically when it ends the phrase or receives contrastive stress: **I don't want to, but I have to.**

## The Homophones

| Word | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **to** | infinitive, direction, recipient or relationship | **to work / to Bogotá / to me** |
| **too** | excess or also | **too late / me too** |
| **two** | number 2 | **two days** |

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Los dos conductores también están demasiado cansados para conducir a Cali. | The two drivers are too tired to drive to Cali too. |

Context and spelling—not the strong sound—separate the meanings.

## Near-Sound Traps: **Tow** and **Toe**

**Tow** and **toe** are homophones pronounced `/toʊ/`, with the long **o** sound. They do not sound like strong **to/too/two** `/tuː/`.

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| La grúa remolcó el carro después de que me lastimé un dedo del pie. | The truck had to tow the car after I hurt my toe. |

Here **to** is `/tə/`, while **tow/toe** are `/toʊ/`.

## Rhyming `/uː/` Words

These are not homophones, but their final vowel helps practice `/uː/`:

- **do** `/duː/`
- **you** `/juː/`
- **who** `/huː/`
- **through** `/θruː/`
- **blue** `/bluː/`
- **knew/new** `/nuː/`

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| ¿Quién sabía que tú también tenías que pasar por las dos puertas azules? | Who knew you had to go through the two blue doors too? |

**Knew** and **new** form another homophone pair inside the sound drill.

## Minimal-Difference Sound Sentences

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Dos sastres cansados intentaron confeccionar dos tutús también. | Two tired tailors tried to tailor two tutus, too. |
| No quiero ir, pero tengo que ir porque los dos estamos demasiado retrasados. | I don't want to go, but I have to because we're both too far behind. |
| ¿Quién sabía que la grúa también tendría que remolcar los dos carros nuevos? | Who knew the tow truck would have to tow the two new cars too? |

These sentences alternate strong **two/too**, weak **to**, final strong **to**, `/toʊ/` in **tow**, and the **new/knew** homophones.
