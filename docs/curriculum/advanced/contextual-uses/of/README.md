# Contextual Uses of **Of**

Spanish **de** often becomes **of**, but English may instead use noun order or **'s**. Many verbs and expressions also require **of** as a fixed connection.

| Section | Spanish | English |
|---|---|---|
| [Core Uses](core-uses/README.md) | El nombre de la tienda está en la taza de café de Ana. | The name of the store is on Ana's cup of coffee. |
| [Fixed Connections](fixed-connections/README.md) | Debido a la lluvia, usamos el bus en vez del carro. | Because of the rain, we took the bus instead of the car. |
| [**Of**, Noun Order, and **'s**](confusion-sets/README.md) | La leche con chocolate de John no es chocolate con leche. | John's chocolate milk isn't milk chocolate. |

## Whole-Topic Mastery Phrases

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Debido a la lluvia, aprovechamos el bus en vez del carro. | Because of the rain, we took advantage of the bus instead of the car. |
| Había oído hablar de Ana y tenía una buena opinión de su manera de enseñar. | I'd heard of Ana and had a good opinion of her way of teaching. |
| La taza de café de John está junto a la leche con chocolate de Ana. | John's cup of coffee is next to Ana's chocolate milk. |
