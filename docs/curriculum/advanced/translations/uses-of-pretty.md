# Uses of **Pretty**

> **It's pretty important to be pretty.**

**Pretty** can describe an attractive appearance or informally express a considerable degree. Its grammatical position reveals which meaning is intended.

## **Pretty** Meaning Attractive

As an adjective, **pretty** means **bonito**, **lindo** or **bello**. It often describes a person, place or thing with a delicate or pleasing appearance.

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| El jardín se ve muy bonito ahora que las flores rosadas están abiertas. | The garden looks very pretty now that the pink flowers are open. |

Here **pretty** follows **looks** and describes the garden.

## **Pretty** Meaning Fairly or Quite

In informal English, **pretty** before another adjective or an adverb means **bastante**, **considerablemente** or, depending on context, **muy**.

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Es bastante importante guardar una copia del contrato porque el original podría perderse. | It's pretty important to keep a copy of the contract because the original could get lost. |

Here **pretty** modifies **important**. It does not describe an attractive appearance.

The strength of degree **pretty** depends partly on context and tone. **Pretty good** can be measured praise, while **pretty serious** can describe a substantial problem. This use is natural in conversation and informal writing; use **fairly**, **quite**, **considerably** or a more precise degree in formal writing.

## Minimal-Difference Teaching Sentence

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Es bastante importante ser bonita. | It's pretty important to be pretty. |

The first **pretty** modifies **important** and means **bastante**. The second follows **be**, describes appearance and means **bonita**. This is an intentionally playful teaching sentence designed to make the two grammatical roles memorable, not a general claim that physical appearance determines a person's importance.

The standard contraction **it's** means **it is**. There is no special informal reduction of **pretty** comparable to **kinda**; the word itself already has an informal degree use.

## Position Changes the Meaning

Compare the two uses in a minimally different pair:

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Laura es bonita. | Laura is pretty. |
| Laura está bastante segura de que aprobó el examen. | Laura is pretty sure she passed the exam. |

In **Laura is pretty**, **pretty** is the adjective after **be**. In **Laura is pretty sure**, it comes before and modifies the adjective **sure**.
