# Uses of **Rather**

**Rather** can express degree, preference, an alternative, or a correction. Its Spanish translation and the grammar that follows it change with each use.

## **Rather** Meaning Fairly or Quite

Before an adjective or adverb, **rather** can mean **bastante**, **algo** or **un tanto**.

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Es bastante importante guardar una copia del contrato porque el original puede perderse. | It is rather important to keep a copy of the contract because the original could get lost. |

The possible loss explains why the matter has a significant degree of importance. This use of **rather** is especially common in British English.

## **Rather** With an Unexpected Positive Quality

Before a positive adjective, **rather** can show that a good quality is stronger or more surprising than expected.

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Pensé que la conferencia sería aburrida, pero resultó bastante interesante y tomé cinco páginas de notas. | I thought the lecture would be boring, but it was rather interesting, and I took five pages of notes. |

The contrast with the speaker's expectation gives **rather interesting** a positive meaning.

## **Rather** Before a Thought or Feeling Verb

Especially in British English, **rather** can soften a personal opinion before verbs such as **think**, **like** or **suspect**.

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Aunque los demás criticaron el diseño, a mí me gusta bastante porque es sencillo y fácil de usar. | Although everyone else criticized the design, I rather like it because it is simple and easy to use. |

The disagreement makes **rather** sound like a polite, measured personal opinion.

## **Rather Too**

**Rather too** means that something exceeds an acceptable limit, although the criticism is expressed mildly.

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Ciento cincuenta dólares me parece un precio algo excesivo para una cena sin bebidas, así que busquemos otro restaurante. | One hundred and fifty dollars is rather too expensive for a dinner without drinks, so let us find another restaurant. |

Here **rather** softens the criticism while **too expensive** establishes that the price is unacceptable.

## **Rather a + Adjective + Noun**

Before a singular countable noun, the usual pattern is **rather a/an + adjective + noun**.

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Fue una sorpresa bastante agradable que todos llegaran temprano a pesar del tráfico. | It was rather a pleasant surprise that everyone arrived early despite the traffic. |

English places **rather** before the article: **rather a pleasant surprise**, not *a rather a pleasant surprise*. **A rather pleasant surprise** is also possible, but it follows a different word order.

## **Would Rather + Base Verb**

Use **would rather + base verb** when the person with the preference performs the action.

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Prefiero revisar el contrato hoy porque mañana no tendré tiempo para leerlo con calma. | I would rather review the contract today because tomorrow I will not have time to read it carefully. |

Although **would rather** contains **would**, Spanish can naturally use either **prefiero** or **preferiría**, depending on context. Do not add **to** or **-ing** after **would rather**.

## **Would Rather A Than B**

Use **would rather + base verb + than + base verb** to compare two actions.

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Preferiría trabajar desde casa en vez de pasar tres horas diarias en el tráfico. | I would rather work from home than spend three hours in traffic every day. |

The same subject performs both actions, so both English verbs remain in the base form: **work** and **spend**.

## **Would Rather Not**

Place **not** after **rather** to make the preferred action negative.

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Preferiría no hablar del aumento durante la cena porque es un asunto privado. | I would rather not discuss the raise during dinner because it is a private matter. |

Use **would rather not discuss**, not *would not rather discuss* or *would rather to not discuss*.

## **Would You Rather...?**

Use **would you rather...?** to ask someone to choose between alternatives.

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| ¿Preferirías presentar el informe hoy o esperar hasta que tengamos las cifras definitivas mañana? | Would you rather present the report today or wait until we have the final figures tomorrow? |

The two alternatives appear after **rather** and **or**.

## **Would Rather + Different Subject**

When someone prefers another person to act, use **would rather + subject + past form** for a present or future situation.

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Preferiría que Laura dirigiera la reunión de mañana porque conoce mejor al cliente. | I would rather Laura led tomorrow's meeting because she knows the client better. |

Although **led** looks past, the meeting is tomorrow. Do not use the same-subject pattern *I would rather Laura lead* in the standard structure taught here.

## **Would Rather + Different Subject + Negative**

For a negative preference about another person's action, put the negative inside the following clause.

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Preferiría que ustedes no enviaran el documento todavía porque contiene cifras sin verificar. | I would rather you did not send the document yet because it contains unverified figures. |

Use **I would rather you did not send**, not *I would not rather you sent*.

## **Would Rather Have + Past Participle**

Use **would rather have + past participle** when the speaker preferred a different action in the past.

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Habría preferido tomar el vuelo de la mañana porque el de la noche fue cancelado. | I would rather have taken the morning flight because the evening flight was canceled. |

The cancellation has already happened, so the preferred alternative is now impossible.

## **Would Rather + Subject + Had + Past Participle**

For a past preference about another person's action, use **would rather + subject + had + past participle**.

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Habría preferido que Carlos me hubiera llamado antes de firmar porque el contrato contenía un error. | I would rather Carlos had called me before signing because the contract contained an error. |

Carlos did not call before he signed, so the sentence expresses dissatisfaction with a completed past event.

## **Rather Than**

**Rather than** introduces the alternative that is not chosen and often means **en vez de**, **en lugar de** or **más que**.

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| En vez de cancelar el proyecto por falta de dinero, redujimos el alcance y terminamos las funciones esenciales. | Rather than cancel the project because of insufficient funding, we reduced its scope and completed the essential features. |

The team chooses reducing the scope instead of canceling the project. Keep compared structures parallel when possible.

## **Not X but Rather Y**

Use **not X but rather Y** to reject one description and replace it with a more accurate one.

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| El retraso no fue causado por falta de esfuerzo, sino por un error en los datos del cliente. | The delay was not caused by a lack of effort but rather by an error in the client's data. |

The second cause replaces the rejected first cause, so **but rather** translates naturally as **sino** or **sino más bien**.

## **Or Rather**

Use **or rather** to correct or make a previous statement more precise.

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| La reunión duró dos horas, o mejor dicho, una hora y cincuenta y ocho minutos. | The meeting lasted two hours, or rather, one hour and fifty-eight minutes. |

The second duration corrects the first with more precise information.

## Sentence-Opening **Rather**

In formal English, **rather** can reject a previous conclusion and introduce the opposite or more accurate interpretation.

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| La caída de las ventas no demuestra que el producto haya fracasado; más bien, indica que necesitamos cambiar la estrategia de publicidad. | The drop in sales does not prove that the product has failed; rather, it indicates that we need to change the advertising strategy. |

Here **rather** means **más bien**, **por el contrario** or **en cambio**, not **bastante**.
