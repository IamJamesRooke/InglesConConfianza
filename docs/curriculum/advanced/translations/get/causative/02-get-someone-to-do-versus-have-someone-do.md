# **Get Someone To Do** Versus **Have Someone Do**

Both patterns can mean arranging for another person to do something, but their forms are different.

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Conseguí que Ana pintara la puerta y le pedí al técnico que arreglara la ventana. | I got Ana to paint the door, and I had the technician fix the window. |

- **get + person + to + verb** often suggests persuasion or effort: **I got Ana to help.**
- **have + person + verb** often suggests an arrangement, request, or instruction: **I had Ana help.**

Don't add **to** after the person with **have**: **I had him do it**, not **I had him to do it**.
