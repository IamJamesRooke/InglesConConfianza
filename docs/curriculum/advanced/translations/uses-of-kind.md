# Uses of **Kind**

> **What kind of person is only kind of kind?**

**Kind** can name a category, describe a considerate person or action, or soften a statement in the expression **kind of**. Its meaning depends on its grammatical role.

## **Kind** Meaning Type

As a noun, **kind** means **tipo**, **clase** or **especie**.

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Este tipo de café crece bien en climas fríos y lluviosos. | This kind of coffee grows well in cold, rainy climates. |

Use **kind of + noun** to identify a category. In questions, **what kind of...?** asks which category something belongs to.

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| ¿Qué tipo de música escucha Natalia cuando está trabajando? | What kind of music does Natalia listen to while she is working? |

After **this** and **that**, use singular **kind**; after **these**, **those** and numbers, use plural **kinds**.

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| La tienda vende tres tipos de pan sin gluten, pero este tipo es el más económico. | The store sells three kinds of gluten-free bread, but this kind is the least expensive. |

## **Kind**, **Sort** and **Type**

**Kind**, **sort** and **type** can all mean **tipo** or **clase**, but their usual tone differs.

- **Kind** is the most neutral everyday choice.
- **Sort** is also common in everyday language, especially in British English, and often sounds slightly more informal.
- **Type** often suggests a precise, official, scientific or technical classification.

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Este tipo de batería está clasificado como residuo peligroso, así que debe llevarse a un punto de recolección especial. | This type of battery is classified as hazardous waste, so it must be taken to a special collection point. |

The formal classification makes **type** especially natural here. In many ordinary contexts, however, the three words overlap: **this kind of problem**, **this sort of problem** and **this type of problem** can all mean **este tipo de problema**.

## **Kind** Meaning Considerate

As an adjective, **kind** means **amable**, **bondadoso** or **considerado**.

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| La vecina fue muy amable conmigo: cuidó a mi perro mientras yo estaba en el hospital. | My neighbor was very kind to me: she looked after my dog while I was in the hospital. |

Use **kind to + person** for the person who receives the considerate treatment.

## **It Is Kind of Someone to Do Something**

Use **it is kind of someone to + base verb** to praise a considerate action.

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Fue muy amable de parte de Andrés traerme el almuerzo cuando supo que no podía salir de la oficina. | It was very kind of Andrés to bring me lunch when he learned that I could not leave the office. |

Here **of Andrés** identifies the person whose action was kind. Do not confuse this structure with the softener **kind of** below.

## **Kind Of** Meaning Somewhat

In informal English, **kind of** before an adjective or verb means **algo**, **un poco**, **más o menos** or **como que**. It makes the statement less direct or less exact.

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| La reunión es algo importante porque allí decidiremos el presupuesto, pero no es obligatorio asistir. | The meeting is kind of important because we will decide the budget there, but attendance is not mandatory. |

**Kind of important** normally weakens **important**. It does not reliably mean **bastante importante**, which can express a stronger degree similar to **quite important**.

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Más o menos entiendo la explicación, pero todavía no podría explicársela a otra persona. | I kind of understand the explanation, but I still couldn't explain it to someone else. |

The second clause shows that the speaker's understanding is incomplete.

## Informal Spoken **Kinda**

**Kinda** represents the informal spoken reduction of **kind of** when it means **algo**, **más o menos** or **como que**.

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Estoy algo cansada, así que creo que me voy a ir a casa temprano. | I'm kinda tired, so I think I'll head home early. |

The standard written form is **kind of**. Use **kinda** in casual messages or when representing relaxed speech, not in formal writing. It is an informal phonetic spelling rather than a standard contraction: **I'm** and **I'll** are standard contractions.

Do not use **kinda** when **kind** names a category. Write **this kind of music**, not *this kinda music*, in standard English.

## **Kind Of** Versus **A Kind Of**

Without an article, informal **kind of** can soften a description. With **a**, **a kind of + noun** identifies a category or describes something approximately as a category.

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| La bebida es un tipo de té fermentado, y su sabor es algo ácido. | The drink is a kind of fermented tea, and its flavor is kind of sour. |

The first **kind of** means **un tipo de**; the second means **algo**. In formal writing, prefer a more precise word such as **somewhat**, **approximately** or **a type of** when it expresses the intended meaning.
