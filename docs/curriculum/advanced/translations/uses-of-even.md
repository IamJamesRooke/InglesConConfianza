# Uses of **Even**

> **Even an even floor can have an odd number of tiles.**

**Even** does not have one fixed translation. Read the complete expression and choose the meaning from context.

## **Even** for Surprising Inclusion

Use **even** to include someone or something that the listener would not expect.

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Todos resolvieron el problema; incluso Laura, que no había asistido a la clase, dio la respuesta correcta. | Everyone solved the problem; even Laura, who had not attended the class, gave the correct answer. |

The surprise is that Laura succeeded despite missing the class.

## **Even If**

Use **even if** when a possible condition will not change the result.

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Incluso si llueve mañana, jugaremos el partido porque el estadio tiene techo. | Even if it rains tomorrow, we will play the game because the stadium has a roof. |

The rain is only a possibility, and the roof guarantees that it will not change the plan.

## **Even Though**

Use **even though** when a fact is real but contrasts strongly with the result.

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Aunque llovió toda la mañana, jugamos el partido porque el estadio tenía techo. | Even though it rained all morning, we played the game because the stadium had a roof. |

Unlike **even if**, this sentence confirms that the rain really happened.

## **Even When**

Use **even when** when the same result continues in a particular or repeated situation.

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Andrés va al trabajo en bicicleta todos los días, incluso cuando llueve. | Andrés rides his bicycle to work every day, even when it rains. |

**Every day** makes it clear that rainy days are included in a repeated habit.

## **Not Even**

Use **not even** when someone or something fails to reach an expected minimum.

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Laura estaba tan enojada que se fue sin despedirse y ni siquiera cerró la puerta. | Laura was so angry that she left without saying goodbye and did not even close the door. |

Closing the door is presented as the minimum expected action.

## **Even** Before a Comparison

Before a comparative, **even** increases the degree of the comparison.

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| El segundo examen fue aún más difícil que el primero, aunque tuvimos una hora adicional para terminarlo. | The second exam was even more difficult than the first, although we had an extra hour to finish it. |

Here **even** intensifies **more difficult**; it does not mean **incluso**.

## **Even So**

Use **even so** to show that a fact did not prevent a contrasting result.

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| El plazo era de solo dos días; aun así, entregamos el proyecto completo y a tiempo. | The deadline was only two days; even so, we delivered the complete project on time. |

**Even so** refers back to the short deadline and introduces the unexpected result.

## **Even As**

Use **even as** to emphasize that two things happen at exactly the same time, often with a surprising contrast.

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Justo cuando el director anunciaba los recortes, la empresa reportó ganancias récord. | Even as the director announced the cuts, the company reported record profits. |

The two simultaneous events create the contrast. Depending on context, **even as** can be **justo cuando**, **al mismo tiempo que** or **incluso mientras**.

## **Even Now** and **Even Then**

Before a time expression, **even** emphasizes that something remains surprising at that time.

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Han pasado veinte años desde el accidente y, aún ahora, Marta evita conducir de noche. | Twenty years have passed since the accident, and even now, Marta avoids driving at night. |

The twenty-year interval makes Marta's continuing reaction surprising. The same pattern gives **even then → aun entonces / incluso en ese momento**.

## **Even** and **Odd** Numbers

An **even number** is divisible by two; an **odd number** is not.

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Doce es un número par porque se puede dividir exactamente entre dos, pero trece es impar. | Twelve is an even number because it can be divided exactly by two, but thirteen is odd. |

Learn the contrast together: **even and odd → par e impar**.

## **Even** Meaning Level

As an adjective, **even** can describe a surface without slopes or irregularities.

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| La mesa se tambalea porque el piso no está nivelado. | The table wobbles because the floor is not even. |

The wobbling shows that **even** describes the physical level of the floor.

## **Even** Meaning Steady

**Even** can describe something that remains regular, constant, or calm instead of fluctuating.

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Aunque estaba nerviosa, Paula mantuvo una voz tranquila y constante durante toda la entrevista. | Although she was nervous, Paula maintained a calm, even voice throughout the interview. |

Her nervousness makes the steadiness of her voice significant. In other contexts, **an even pace** or **an even temperature** can mean **un ritmo constante** or **una temperatura estable**.

## **Even** Meaning Equally Distributed

**Even** can mean that an amount is divided or distributed equally.

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Dividimos las veinte tareas en partes iguales, así que cada miembro del equipo recibió cinco. | We made an even division of the twenty tasks, so each team member received five. |

The equal result identifies **even** as **igual**, **uniforme** or **equitativo**, not **nivelado**.

## **Even** Meaning Equal

**Even** can mean that neither side is ahead.

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Cada equipo ha ganado dos partidos, así que están empatados antes de la final. | Each team has won two games, so they are even before the final. |

The equal number of victories establishes the meaning.

## **An Even Chance**

An **even chance** means that two possible outcomes are equally likely.

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Según el médico, hay las mismas probabilidades de que el tratamiento funcione o no funcione. | According to the doctor, there is an even chance that the treatment will work or fail. |

The two equally probable outcomes establish the fifty-fifty meaning.

## **An Even Amount**

Before a round number or amount, **even** can mean **exactly**, with no fraction or remainder.

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Después de pagar todos los cargos, la cuenta quedó en cien dólares exactos, sin centavos adicionales. | After all the fees were paid, the bill came to an even one hundred dollars, with no additional cents. |

Here **even** means **exactos** or **redondos**, not **pares**.

## **Even Out**

**Even out** means to reduce a difference and make something more balanced.

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Trabajaremos menos horas el viernes para equilibrar la carga de trabajo después de tres días muy largos. | We will work fewer hours on Friday to even out the workload after three very long days. |

The shorter Friday compensates for the longer days.

## **Even the Score**

As a verb, **even** can make a score, record, or competition equal.

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| El equipo visitante perdía dos a uno, pero marcó en el último minuto para empatar el marcador. | The visiting team was losing two to one, but it scored in the final minute to even the score. |

The final goal changes unequal scores into equal scores.

## **Get Even**

**Get even with someone** means to retaliate for something that person did.

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Tomás dañó mi bicicleta a propósito, pero no voy a vengarme de él dañando la suya. | Tomás damaged my bicycle on purpose, but I am not going to get even with him by damaging his. |

The repeated act of damage makes the idea of retaliation explicit.

## **We Are Even**

**We are even** means that neither person owes the other anything.

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Tú pagaste la cena y yo pagué el taxi por el mismo valor, así que estamos a mano. | You paid for dinner and I paid the same amount for the taxi, so we are even. |

The equal payments settle the debt between the two people.

## **Break Even**

In business, **break even** means that income and costs are equal, so there is neither a profit nor a loss.

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Producir el evento cuesta cinco millones de pesos, así que debemos recibir cinco millones por la venta de entradas para quedar en punto de equilibrio. | The event costs five million pesos to produce, so we must take in five million pesos from ticket sales to break even. |

The equal cost and income establish the break-even point.
