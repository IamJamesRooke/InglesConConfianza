# **Kind**, **Sort** and **Type**

**Kind**, **sort** and **type** can all mean **tipo** or **clase**, but their usual tone differs.

- **Kind** is the most neutral everyday choice.
- **Sort** is also common in everyday language, especially in British English, and often sounds slightly more informal.
- **Type** often suggests a precise, official, scientific or technical classification.

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Este tipo de batería está clasificado como residuo peligroso, así que debe llevarse a un punto de recolección especial. | This type of battery is classified as hazardous waste, so it must be taken to a special collection point. |

The formal classification makes **type** especially natural here. In many ordinary contexts, however, the three words overlap: **this kind of problem**, **this sort of problem** and **this type of problem** can all mean **este tipo de problema**.
