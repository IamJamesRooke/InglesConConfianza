# Translations of Spanish **Poder**

Spanish **poder** can describe ability, permission, possibility, opportunity, successful effort, or power. English chooses among **can, could, be able to, may, might, be allowed to, get to, manage to**, and **power** according to meaning, time, and clause shape.

This folder is both human-readable and database-ready. Each Markdown file owns one translation concept; every nested YAML mapping is an independently trackable form-and-meaning edge. A lesson can therefore teach **puedo** and **puede** while the application records two exposures to the same **present ability with poder** concept.

## High-value contrasts

| Contrast | Teaching decision |
|---|---|
| **can** vs **be able to** | Use **can** where the modal has a form; use **be able to** after **to**, with perfect forms, and when another tense must be built. |
| **could** vs **was able to / managed to** | **Could** describes general past ability. A particular successful action normally uses **was able to** or **managed to**. |
| ability vs permission vs possibility | The same form of **poder** can describe capability, permission, or likelihood. Track these as different concepts. |
| **can** vs **may / might** | **Can** often describes a general possibility; **may** and **might** normally describe one uncertain event. |
| **could** vs **could have** | **Could** can concern the present or a condition; **could have** points to a past possibility or unrealized past ability. |
| **get to** vs **have got to** | **Get to** is an opportunity; **have got to** is an obligation and belongs with **tener que / have to**. |
| **may be** vs **maybe** | **May be** is a verb phrase; **maybe** is an adverb. Spanish may use **puede que** or [**quizá**](../quizá/README.md). |
| **power** vs **authority** | **Power** is broad control or influence; **authority** is an official right to decide or act. |
| **can't** vs **may not** | **Can't** normally marks inability or impossibility. **May not** can mark uncertain non-occurrence or formal prohibition. |
| **Can you...?** ability vs request | Keep the same words as separate concepts because the communicative job changes what the learner is practicing. |
| **Can't you...?** vs **Can you not...?** | **Can't you...?** is the ordinary negative ability question and often carries surprise. **Can you not...?** is marked and may instead mean “please stop.” |

In ordinary speech, affirmative **can** is often reduced while negative **can't** is stressed. Pronunciation is teaching guidance, not a separate translation object.

## Preserved synthesis examples

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Puedo ayudar hoy, quiero poder ayudar mañana y todavía no he podido hablar con Ana. | I can help today, want to be able to help tomorrow, and still haven't been able to speak with Ana. |
| De niño podía nadar, y ayer logré cruzar el río por primera vez. | As a child, I could swim, and yesterday I was able to cross the river for the first time. |
| Hoy puedo trabajar desde casa, pero tengo que conectarme a las ocho. | Today I get to work from home, but I've got to log in at eight. |
| Quizá Ana esté en casa; puede que esté enferma. | Maybe Ana is home; she may be sick. |
| Podría ayudar hoy, pero habría podido ayudar ayer si me hubiera llamado. | I could help today, but I could have helped yesterday if you had called me. |
| No he podido llamarla hoy, pero podré hacerlo mañana y podría ayudarla si todavía me necesita. | I haven't been able to call her today, but I'll be able to tomorrow and could help her if she still needs me. |
| La directora tiene la autoridad para decidir, pero no tiene el poder de cambiar la ley. | The director has the authority to decide, but she doesn't have the power to change the law. |
| De niña podía nadar, y ayer logré cruzar el río aunque la corriente estaba fuerte. | As a child, I could swim, and yesterday I managed to cross the river although the current was strong. |
| Sé nadar, puedo nadar hoy y ayer logré cruzar el río; quizá mañana pueda volver a cruzarlo si me dan permiso. | I know how to swim, can swim today, and managed to cross the river yesterday; maybe I'll be able to cross it again tomorrow if I'm given permission. |
| Ayer pude visitar el museo, pero no alcancé a ver la exposición nueva. | Yesterday I got to visit the museum, but I didn't get to see the new exhibition. |
| ¿Podría entrar? Me dijeron que hoy sí me permiten visitar a Ana. | Could I come in? They told me I'm allowed to visit Ana today. |
| Puede que llueva mañana; Bogotá puede ser fría y quizá tengamos que quedarnos en casa. | It might rain tomorrow; Bogotá can be cold, and maybe we'll have to stay home. |
| La directora tiene la autoridad para decidir, pero no tiene el poder de cambiar la ley; sin electricidad, además, el sistema queda sin energía. | The director has the authority to decide, but she doesn't have the power to change the law; without electricity, the system also has no power. |
