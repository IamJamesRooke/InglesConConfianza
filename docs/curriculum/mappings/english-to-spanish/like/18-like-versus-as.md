# **Like** Versus **As**

Use **like** for similarity. Use **as** for a real role, function, or the way an action is performed.

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Carlos trabaja como gerente y habla como su antiguo jefe. | Carlos works as a manager and speaks like his former boss. |

- **as a manager**: manager is Carlos's real role;
- **like his former boss**: his manner resembles the former boss's manner.

Before a complete idea, formal edited English traditionally prefers **as/as if/as though**:

- **Do it as I showed you.**
- **It looks as if it's going to rain.**

Conversational **Do it like I showed you** and **It looks like it's going to rain** are extremely common.
