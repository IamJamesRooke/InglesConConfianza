# General Conditions

Use a present form in both parts when one result normally follows from a condition. This pattern is traditionally called the **zero conditional**.

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Si tengo suficiente tiempo, termino el informe con cuidado. | If I have enough time, I finish the report carefully. |
| Si presionas este botón, la pantalla se apaga. | If you press this button, the screen turns off. |

The sentence describes a routine, rule, or dependable result. **When** can often replace **if** when the condition is expected rather than uncertain:

**When you press this button, the screen turns off.**
