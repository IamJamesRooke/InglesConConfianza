# Related Pronouns: **Some, Any, No**, and **Every**

The determiner families also build indefinite pronouns.

| Family | Spanish: people | English: people | Spanish: things | English: things |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| some | alguien | somebody / someone | algo | something |
| any | alguien...? | anybody / anyone | algo...? | anything |
| no | nadie | nobody / no one | nada | nothing |
| every | todo el mundo / todos | everybody / everyone | todo | everything |

Compare:

| With a noun: determiner | Without a noun: pronoun |
|---|---|
| some person | somebody |
| any object | anything |
| no person | nobody |
| every object | everything |

The compounds are written as one word except **no one**.

In questions, Spanish can use **alguien** or **algo** where English normally uses the **any** family:

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| ¿Hay alguien aquí? | Is anybody here? |
| ¿Necesitas algo? | Do you need anything? |

In ordinary negative English, use **not + any** or a negative pronoun, not both:

- **I don't know anybody.**
- **I know nobody.**
- Not: *I don't know nobody.*

The detailed affirmative, question, and negative patterns belong to the related lessons under [indefinite pronouns](../../pronouns/indefinite).
