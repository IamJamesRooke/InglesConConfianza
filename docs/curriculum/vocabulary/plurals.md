# Plurals

Regular and high-frequency irregular English noun plurals.
## Regular and Irregular Plurals

Most foundational countable nouns add **-s** in the plural:

| Singular | Plural |
|---|---|
| book | books |
| question | questions |
| car | cars |
| friend | friends |

Some nouns already in this curriculum require a spelling change:

| Pattern | Singular | Plural |
|---|---|---|
| add **-es** after **-ss** | address | addresses |
| consonant + **y → -ies** | company | companies |
| consonant + **y → -ies** | family | families |
| **-fe → -ves** in this word | wife | wives |

After a vowel + **y**, simply add **-s**: **key → keys**.

Several essential people nouns are irregular:

| Spanish | Singular | Plural |
|---|---|---|
| persona / personas, gente | person | people |
| niño / niños | child | children |
| hombre / hombres | man | men |
| mujer / mujeres | woman | women |

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Hay una persona en la oficina. | There is one person in the office. |
| Hay tres personas en la oficina. | There are three people in the office. |
| La mujer tiene dos hijos. | The woman has two children. |

Do not use *childs, mans,* or *womans*. **People** is the normal conversational plural of **person**.
