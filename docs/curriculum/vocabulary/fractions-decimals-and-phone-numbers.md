# Fractions, Decimals, and Phone Numbers

Common number formats that are read differently from ordinary whole quantities.
## Decimals and Phone Numbers

Number formats are not always read like ordinary quantities.

### Alternative reading of hundreds

For 1,500, both forms are natural:

- **one thousand five hundred**
- **fifteen hundred**

The second pattern is common for prices, addresses, and numbers between 1,100 and 9,999 that divide naturally into hundreds.

### Decimals

Say **point**, then read each digit separately:

**3.14159 → three point one four one five nine**

Spanish decimal comma normally becomes a decimal point in English writing: **3,14 → 3.14**.

### Phone numbers

Read phone numbers digit by digit:

**5718 → five seven one eight**

Zero may be pronounced **zero** or **oh**. Repeated digits may be read separately or with **double**: **55 → five five / double five**.

## Fractions

Use **half** for one of two equal parts. Use an ordinal number for many other fractions:

| Fraction | Spanish | English |
|---|---|---|
| 1/2 | la mitad | half / one half |
| 1/3 | un tercio | a third / one third |
| 1/4 | un cuarto | a quarter / one fourth |

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Leí la mitad del libro. | I read half the book. / I read half of the book. |
| Leí un tercio del libro. | I read a third of the book. |
| Leí un cuarto del libro. | I read a quarter of the book. |

Do not normally say *a half of the book*. Use **half the book** or **half of the book**.

For a numerator larger than one, make the denominator plural: **two thirds, three quarters**.
