# Months and Dates

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| enero | January |
| febrero | February |
| marzo | March |
| abril | April |
| mayo | May |
| junio | June |
| julio | July |
| agosto | August |
| septiembre | September |
| octubre | October |
| noviembre | November |
| diciembre | December |

English months begin with a capital letter. Use **in** with a month or year and **on** with a specific date:

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| La reunión es en agosto. | The meeting is in August. |
| El curso empieza en 2027. | The course starts in 2027. |
| La reunión es el 28 de agosto. | The meeting is on August 28th. |

In American English, say **August twenty-eighth** for **August 28th**.

## Saying a complete date

American English normally puts the month before the ordinal day:

| Written date | Spoken date |
|---|---|
| Thursday, January 21, 1999 | Thursday, January twenty-first, nineteen ninety-nine |
| Friday, July 4, 2025 | Friday, July fourth, twenty twenty-five |

Ordinal endings are common in informal date writing—**January 21st**—but are usually omitted when the complete date is written formally: **January 21, 1999**.

British English commonly puts the ordinal day before the month:

| Written date | Spoken date |
|---|---|
| Thursday, 21 January 1999 | Thursday, the twenty-first of January, nineteen ninety-nine |

Both orders are correct. Stay consistent within one document.

## Complete dates with **on**

Use only one **on** before the complete date:

- The meeting is **on Thursday, January 21st, 1999**.
- Laura arrived **on Friday, July 4th**.
- Not: *on Thursday, on January 21st*

When the day of the week is included, separate it with a comma. In the American order, also place a comma before the year.

Numeric dates such as **01/02/2026** are ambiguous: Americans may read this as January 2, while many other readers understand February 1. Spell out the month when clarity matters.
