For shades, use the supplemental **claro** lesson: **azul claro** is **light blue**, while **verde oscuro** is **dark green**. The English shade word comes before the color. For example, **Laura lleva una chaqueta verde oscuro. → Laura is wearing a dark green jacket.**

### Register and usage notes

- **Inexpensive** is more neutral than **cheap**; **cheap** can also suggest poor quality. **Ese apartamento es barato. → That apartment is cheap.**
- **Dear** can mean expensive in some contexts, but it also commonly means loved or addressed in a letter. **Este apartamento es caro. → This apartment is expensive.**
- **Brief** is a more formal alternative to **short**. **El descanso fue corto. → The break was short.**
- **Lengthy** emphasizes extended detail or duration. **La reunión fue larga. → The meeting was long.**
- **Slow** describes a person, vehicle, process, or movement with low speed. **El carro es lento. → The car is slow.**
- **Easy** describes a low level of difficulty; **simple** means uncomplicated or having few parts. **La primera parte es fácil, pero la segunda es difícil. → The first part is easy, but the second is hard.** **Es una pregunta fácil. → It is a simple question.**
- **Difficult** and **hard** overlap when describing difficulty. **Este trabajo es difícil. → This work is difficult.** English **hard** can also describe physical hardness.
- **Free** is the ordinary choice for **gratuito**; **free of charge** is explicit or formal, and **complimentary** describes something offered as a courtesy. **El evento es gratuito. → The event is free.** **La entrada es gratuita. → Admission is free of charge.** **El hotel ofrece un desayuno gratuito. → The hotel offers a complimentary breakfast.**
- **Important** is the everyday choice. **Major** emphasizes size, seriousness, or importance, while Spanish **significativo** gives English **significant** when impact or meaning is emphasized. **Esta reunión es importante. → This meeting is important.** **La decisión tuvo un efecto significativo. → The decision had a significant effect.** **Es un problema importante para la ciudad. → It is a major problem for the city.**
- Everyday English often prefers **wrong** where formal language uses **incorrect**. Spanish likewise distinguishes **incorrecto** and **equivocado**. **Ese número está equivocado. → That number is wrong.**
- **Joven** normally means **young**. Use **youthful** for **juvenil** and legal **minor** for **menor de edad**. **La empresa es joven. → The company is young.** **Tiene una energía juvenil. → She has youthful energy.** **El joven es menor de edad. → The young man is a minor.**

These direct or near-synonymous choices remain supplemental vocabulary rather than separate mapping families.

## Core vocabulary

### Translations of Spanish **Correcto**

- **Correcto** → **Correct** and **Right**

#### **Correcto** → **Correct** and **Right**

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| correcto / correcta | correct / right |

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Tu respuesta es correcta. | Your answer is correct. |

Use **right** when evaluating an answer or saying a person is correct; use **correct** for accuracy.

- **Grande** → **Big** and **Large**

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| grande | big / large |

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Vivimos en una casa pequeña, pero el jardín es grande. | We live in a small house, but the yard is big. |

### Spanish **Junto**

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Trabajamos juntos. | We work together. |
| Siéntate junto a mí. | Sit next to me. |
| Envié el formulario junto con la copia. | I sent the form along with the copy. |
| Las dos piezas están juntas. | The two pieces are joined together. |

**Junto** can become **together, next to**, or **along with**, depending on the preposition and agreement.

#### **Juntos** → **Together**

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| juntos | together |
| juntos / juntas | together |

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Laura y Diana están juntas en la oficina. | Laura and Diana are together in the office. |

**Juntos** describes people or things forming one group.

### Spanish **Nuevo**

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Compré un teléfono nuevo. | I bought a new phone. |
| Necesitamos un plan nuevo. | We need a new/different plan. |
| Es nuevo en la empresa. | He is new to the company. |
| De nuevo llegó tarde. | Once again, she arrived late. |

**Nuevo** can become **new, different**, or **new to** a place; **de nuevo** means **again**.

### Spanish **Próximo**

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|

**Próximo** can become **next, near**, or **upcoming**, depending on time and distance.

### Translations of Spanish **Rápido**

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|

**Fast** commonly describes speed; **quick** often describes a short amount of time or a rapid action.

### Spanish **Siguiente**

| Spanish | English |

**Blanco** can become **white, blank**, or **target**, and **quedarse en blanco** means one's mind goes blank.
