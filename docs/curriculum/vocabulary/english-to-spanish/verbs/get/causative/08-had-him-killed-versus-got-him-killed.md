# **Had Him Killed** Versus **Got Him Killed**

Both expressions connect one person's actions to another person's death, but they normally assign intention differently.

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| El jefe mandó a matar a Leo; el informante causó la muerte de Leo al revelar dónde estaba. | The boss had Leo killed; the informant got Leo killed by revealing his location. |

- **He had him killed.** — he deliberately ordered or arranged for someone to kill him.
- **He got him killed.** — something he did caused or led to the killing, often unintentionally.

The structure explains the difference:

- **have + person + killed** focuses on arranging the action.
- **get + person + killed** focuses on causing the resulting state or event.

Context can make **got him killed** deliberate, but it doesn't automatically mean that he gave the order. The same pattern appears without another person: **He got himself killed** means his own actions led to his death, not that he hired someone to kill him.
