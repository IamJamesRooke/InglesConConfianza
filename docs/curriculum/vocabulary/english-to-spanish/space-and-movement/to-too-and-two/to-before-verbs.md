# **To** Before a Verb

> **I want to learn when to use _to_—and when not to.**

Before a verb, **to** usually builds the full infinitive or expresses purpose. Other English patterns require the bare infinitive without **to**.

## Full Infinitive Versus Bare Infinitive

| Form | Example |
|---|---|
| full infinitive | **to do** |
| bare infinitive | **do** |

Compare:

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Quiero hacerlo. | I want **to do** it. |
| Puedo hacerlo. | I can **do** it. |

Learn the complete chunk selected by the first word: **want to do**, but **can do**.

## First Verb + Full Infinitive

Many common verbs select **to + base verb**.

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Quiero aprender a conducir antes de buscar un trabajo nuevo. | I want to learn to drive before I look for a new job. |
| Decidimos esperar porque esperábamos recibir más información. | We decided to wait because we expected to receive more information. |
| Ella prometió llamar, pero se le olvidó hacerlo. | She promised to call, but she forgot to do it. |

High-use chunks include:

- **want / need / would like to**
- **plan / decide / choose to**
- **hope / expect / intend to**
- **try / learn / manage to**
- **agree / promise / offer to**
- **refuse / fail / forget to**
- **afford / seem / appear to**

The useful unit is the complete phrase—**decide to do**, **manage to do**, **refuse to do**—not an abstract rule applied to every verb.

## Another Person + Full Infinitive

When the second action belongs to another person, place that person before the full infinitive.

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Quiero que lo hagas. | I want you to do it. |
| Necesito que Laura me llame hoy. | I need Laura to call me today. |
| Diles que esperen afuera. | Tell them to wait outside. |
| Le pedimos al cliente que firmara las dos copias. | We asked the client to sign both copies. |

Spanish often uses **que + another verb**. English uses **person + to + base verb** after words such as **want, need, would like, ask, tell, expect, allow, advise, invite, remind**, and **encourage**.

Some causative patterns use the bare infinitive instead:

- **They made me wait**, not *made me to wait*.
- **Let me explain**, not *let me to explain*.
- **Help me carry this** and **help me to carry this** are both possible.

## Purpose: **Para Hacer → To Do**

Use the full infinitive when the same person performs an action for a purpose.

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Voy a la tienda para comprar dos cuadernos. | I'm going to the store to buy two notebooks. |
| Llamé para confirmar la hora. | I called to confirm the time. |
| Salimos temprano para evitar el tráfico. | We left early to avoid the traffic. |

Use **in order to** when the purpose needs emphasis: **We left early in order to avoid the traffic.**

Do not use *for + base verb*: **to buy**, not *for buy*.

When the purpose belongs to another person, use **so that**:

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Hablo despacio para que puedas entenderme. | I speak slowly so that you can understand me. |

## Description + Full Infinitive

The full infinitive can explain an evaluation, reaction or readiness.

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Es importante revisar las dos cifras. | It's important to check both figures. |
| Me alegra verte. | I'm happy to see you. |
| Estamos listos para comenzar. | We're ready to begin. |
| El informe es difícil de entender. | The report is difficult to understand. |

## Noun or Pronoun + Full Infinitive

The full infinitive can identify what should or can be done with someone or something.

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Necesito algo para comer y un lugar donde sentarme. | I need something to eat and a place to sit. |
| No hay nadie con quien hablar. | There's no one to talk to. |
| Tengo dos correos para responder. | I've got two emails to answer. |

The final preposition in **someone to talk to** is natural English and should not be removed.

## Question Word + Full Infinitive

Use forms such as **what to do**, **where to go**, **how to start**, and **who to ask** when the person making the decision is understood.

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| No sé qué hacer, adónde ir ni a quién pedirle ayuda. | I don't know what to do, where to go, or who to ask for help. |

## **Too** and **Enough + Full Infinitive**

The full infinitive can express the consequence of a limit.

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Estoy demasiado cansado para conducir. | I'm too tired to drive. |
| Laura tiene la edad suficiente para conducir. | Laura is old enough to drive. |

**Too tired to drive** means the tiredness prevents driving. **Old enough to drive** means the required degree has been reached.

## Obligation and Plans

Several common structures contain a full infinitive:

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Tengo que hacerlo. | I have to do it. |
| Voy a hacerlo. | I'm going to do it. |
| Se supone que debo hacerlo. | I'm supposed to do it. |
| Estoy a punto de hacerlo. | I'm about to do it. |

Informal speech commonly reduces **have got to → gotta** and **going to → gonna**:

- **I've gotta do it** represents very informal speech.
- **I'm gonna do it** represents very informal speech.

Use **I've got to do it** and **I'm going to do it** in standard writing.

## When English Uses No **To**

Use the bare infinitive after:

- modals: **can, could, may, might, must, should, will, would**;
- **do, does**, and **did** in questions, negatives and emphasis;
- commands and **let's**;
- **let** and **make**;
- **had better** and **would rather**;
- **why not**.

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Puedo hacerlo, pero preferiría esperar. | I can do it, but I'd rather wait. |
| ¿Lo hiciste? | Did you do it? |
| Hagámoslo ahora. | Let's do it now. |
| Será mejor que te vayas. | You'd better leave. |
| ¿Por qué no llamamos? | Why not call? |

Do not insert **to**: *can to do*, *did to do*, *let me to do*, and *would rather to do* are incorrect.

## Perception: Complete Event Versus Ongoing Action

After **see, hear, watch**, and **feel + person**, the bare infinitive presents a complete event; **-ing** presents an action in progress.

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Vi a Laura cruzar la calle de un lado al otro. | I saw Laura cross the street. |
| Vi a Laura cruzando la calle cuando sonó mi teléfono. | I saw Laura crossing the street when my phone rang. |

## Negative Full Infinitive

Put **not** before the full infinitive.

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Decidí no llamar porque ya era medianoche. | I decided not to call because it was already midnight. |
| Le pedí que no llamara después de las diez. | I asked her not to call after ten. |

## Leaving the Repeated Verb Unsaid

When the action is already clear, English can keep **to** and omit the repeated verb.

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| —¿Vas a llamar a Laura? —Quiero hacerlo, pero no sé si tenga tiempo. | “Are you going to call Laura?” “I want to, but I don't know whether I'll have time.” |

The remaining **to** represents the understood phrase **to call Laura**.

## Three Mastery Sentences

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Quiero que Laura decida qué hacer para evitar tener que trabajar hasta tarde. | I want Laura to decide what to do to avoid having to work late. |
| Puedo ayudarla a terminar, pero preferiría no hacerla quedarse si está demasiado cansada para trabajar. | I can help her finish, but I'd rather not make her stay if she's too tired to work. |
| Le pedí que no llamara, pero quería hacerlo para decirme dónde ir y con quién hablar. | I asked her not to call, but she wanted to, to tell me where to go and who to talk to. |

Together, these sentences contrast full and bare infinitives, another person, purpose, obligation, **-ing** after **avoid**, negative infinitives, **too...to**, omitted repeated verbs, and question-word patterns.
