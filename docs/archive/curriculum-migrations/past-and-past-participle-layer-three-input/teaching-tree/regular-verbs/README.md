# Regular Past and Past Participles

Regular verbs use the same written form for the past and past participle. The teaching distinction is the final **-ed** sound: `/d/`, `/t/`, or `/ɪd/`.

## Regular Verbs in the Past

Regular English verbs form the past with **-ed** or **-d**. The spelling stays the same for every subject.

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| trabajé, trabajaste, trabajó, trabajamos, trabajaron | **worked** |

**Trabajé ayer.**<br>
I worked yesterday.

**Trabajaron ayer.**<br>
They worked yesterday.

The subject changes, but **worked** does not.

## Pronunciation rule

Use this simple course rule:

> **Si no termina en -ted o -ded, no pronunciamos la e.**

That produces three possible endings:

| Written ending | Pronunciation | Extra syllable? |
|---|---|---|
| most other **-ed** forms | `/t/` or `/d/` | no |
| **-ted** or **-ded** | `/ɪd/` | yes |

## Regular Past Participles

Regular past participles end in written **-ed** or **-d**. They use the same spelling and pronunciation as the simple past.

The participle normally appears with a helping verb rather than standing alone:

**He trabajado aquí.**<br>
I have worked here.

Use the same course pronunciation rule:

> **Si no termina en -ted o -ded, no pronunciamos la e.**

| Written ending | Pronunciation | Extra syllable? |
|---|---|---|
| most other **-ed** forms | `/t/` or `/d/` | no |
| **-ted** or **-ded** | `/ɪd/` | yes |

## Forming the Regular Past

## Usually add **-ed**

| Base verb | Past |
|---|---|
| work | **worked** |
| ask | **asked** |
| finish | **finished** |
| help | **helped** |

## If the verb ends in **-e**, add **-d**

| Base verb | Past |
|---|---|
| like | **liked** |
| arrive | **arrived** |
| live | **lived** |

## If the verb ends in consonant + **y**, change **y** to **i** and add **-ed**

| Base verb | Past |
|---|---|
| study | **studied** |
| try | **tried** |

If a vowel comes before **y**, simply add **-ed**:

| Base verb | Past |
|---|---|
| stay | **stayed** |
| show | **showed** |

The spelling changes, but these forms still follow the same pronunciation rule.
