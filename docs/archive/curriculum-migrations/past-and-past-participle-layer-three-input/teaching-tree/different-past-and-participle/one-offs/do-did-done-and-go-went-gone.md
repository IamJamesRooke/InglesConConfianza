# **Do → Did → Done** and **Go → Went → Gone**

These high-frequency auxiliaries and verbs need direct retrieval.

## **Do → Did → Done** and the Prefix Principle

Many compounds inherit the complete irregular chain of the final verb.

| Model | Inherited examples |
|---|---|
| do → did → done | undo → undid → undone; overdo → overdid → overdone |
| take → took → taken | overtake, retake, undertake |
| come → came → come | become, overcome |
| stand → stood → stood | understand, withstand |
| tell → told → told | foretell |
| see → saw → seen | foresee |
| write → wrote → written | rewrite |
| draw → drew → drawn | withdraw |
| give → gave → given | forgive |
| get → got → got/gotten | forget |

This is why counting every compound creates inflated “370+ verb” lists. Learn the head family and attach the prefix.

**I rewrote what I had written and redid what I had done.**

## Irregular Past: **Do → Did**

| Spanish | Base verb | Past |
|---|---|---|
| hacer, hizo | **do** | **did** |

**Hice mi tarea después de cenar.**<br>
I did my homework after dinner.

**Did** ends with the sound `/d/`.

## Irregular Past Participles: **Done** and **Gone**

Learn these changes together:

- **do → done**
- **go → gone**

Both participles finish with the sound `/n/`.

| Spanish | Base form | Past participle |
|---|---|---|
| hacer, hecho | **do** | **done** |
| ir, ido | **go** | **gone** |

**He hecho mi tarea y he ido a la oficina.**<br>
I have done my homework and gone to the office.

The same participle appears in **go back**:

| Spanish | Base form | Past participle |
|---|---|---|
| regresar, regresado | **go back** | **gone back** |

**He regresado a la oficina para buscar mis llaves.**<br>
I have gone back to the office to look for my keys.

## Irregular Past: **Go → Went**

| Spanish | Base form | Past |
|---|---|---|
| ir, fue | **go** | **went** |

**Fui a la oficina después del almuerzo.**<br>
I went to the office after lunch.

The same change appears in **go back**:

| Spanish | Base form | Past |
|---|---|---|
| regresar, regresó | **go back** | **went back** |

**Regresé a la oficina después de la reunión.**<br>
I went back to the office after the meeting.

**Went** ends with the sound `/t/`.

## **Be → Was/Were → Been** and Other One-Offs

These high-frequency chains do not belong to a useful rhyme family.

| Base | Past | Participle | Spanish |
|---|---|---|---|
| be | was / were | been | ser / estar |
| can | could | — | poder |
| do | did | done | hacer |
| go | went | gone | ir |

**Can/could** has no participle. Use **been able to**: **I have been able to help**.

Other modal pairs—**may/might, shall/should**, and **will/would**—are historically related forms, but the second word is not an ordinary past form in every modern use. Modals have no participles; use an equivalent expression when one is required: **had to**, **been allowed to**, or **been willing to**.

**We were there yesterday, and we have been there before—but Laura had already gone.**
